Variance

Variance

  • Variance (\(Var[X]\)) is a measure of the spread of a distribution relative to its expected value (Dowdy et al. 2004)

    • is the measure of variability in a single variable;
    • is the average squared distance from the mean;
    • is in squared units (has no intuitive meaning);

  • Standard Deviation (SD) is the squared root of the variance:

    • \(\text{SD} = \sqrt{\text{Variance}}\)
    • The standard deviation is
      • in the same units of measurement as the measured variable

Variance: population vs. sample

Population variance, \(\sigma^2\)

\[ \sigma^2 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N [x_i - \mu]^2 }{N}, \]

where \(\mu\) is the population mean:

\(\mu = {\sum_{i=1}^N x_i }/{N}\)

Only used with the population

Standard Deviation:

\(\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2}\)

Sample variance, \(s^2\)

\[ s^2 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N [x_i - \bar{x}]^2 }{N-1}, \]

where \(\bar{x}\) is the sample mean:

\(\bar{x} = {\sum_{i=1}^N x_i }/{N}\)

Most commonly used in statistics

Sample Standard Deviation:

\(s = \sqrt{s^2}\)

Standard Deviation

represents the “typical” deviation of observations from the mean.

If the distribution is normal:

  • 68.3% obs. are in +-1 SD;
  • 95.4% obs. are in +-2 SD;
  • 99.7% obs. are in +-3 SD;

The standard deviation is in the same units of measurement as the measured variable.

Not every distribution is normal, but SD can be the same

Note

A good description of distribution should include

  • Central tendency;

  • SD (Variance);

  • Description of the shape;

    • modes

    • symmetry / skew

Example: Measuring stunting

Important

Stunting is the impaired growth and development that children experience from poor nutrition, repeated infection, and inadequate psycho-social stimulation

  • Stunting in the first 1000 days of life has negative consequences on the child’s physical and mental development.
  • See: WHO. Stunting in a nutshell and more information on Hunger and Undernourishment.

Global Stunting

Source: What is childhood stunting?

How to measure if the child is stunted?

WHO has developed:

  • Child Growth standards

The Height-for-age standard stipulates:

  • Lower boundaries of heights without stunting
  • at all ages 0 days - 12 years.

Length/Height-for-age in boys and girls

How this standard is made?

WHO’s data collection:

WHO Child Growth Standards

  • Random sample of children was followed in each country for several years.

  • Children were regularly screened and measured.

Simplified methodology:

For every age group:

  • Mean/median and standard deviation were calculated.

  • Any child with height-for-age within two standard deviations from the mean is considered non-stunting.

  • For a regular population, 95% of children are not stunted.

How this standard is made?

The stanard summary

WHO’s data on the healthy population defined

  • probability distributions of heights for age and gender of children

by:

  • Calculating mean, height at age and its standard deviation

  • Assuming that the distribution is normal

Stunting was defined as:

  • actual height 2 standard deviation below the mean of a healthy population.

The (Satandard) Normal Distribution

Is the symmetric, bell-shaped normal distribution, whit:

  • \(\mu = 0\),

  • \(\text{SD} = 1\).

  • X axis corresponds to the z-scores.

Z-score

The Z-score of an observation \(x_i\) is the number of standard deviations \(s\) that it (\(x_i\)) falls above or below the mean \(\bar x\).

We can compute a z-score of every observation \(x_i\) in our sample. For that we need:

  • sample mean \(\bar x\), and
  • sample standard deviation, \(s\).

\[ Z_i = \frac{x_i-\bar x}{s} \]

Please memorize it!

Source: (Diez et al. 2022).

Why do we need it?

  • to compare distributions, or
  • locate an individual observations \(i\) on / relative to a distribution.
  • to determine % of population below and above such an observation \(i\)

Image source: (Diez et al. 2022).

Standardization

The process of converting any value \(i\) of any distribution to a standard normal distribution.

\[ Z_i = \frac{x_i - \bar x}{s} \]

where:

  • \(\bar x\) is the mean, and
  • \(s\) is the SD, and
  • \(x_i\) is the observation \(i\).

Example (exam): det. stunting status (1)

Stunting is defined as having height 2 standard deviations lower then the average height of a healthy child of the same gender and age.

We’ve measured that:

  • a 5 years old girl is 97 cm tall

Can we consider her suffering from stunting?

According to the Child Growth Standards about height-for-age, the height of a healthy girl at the age of 5 years old:

  • is normally distributed with
  • the mean \(\mu = 109\) cm and
  • the SD \(s = 4.73\) cm.

Example: determine stunting status (2)

Computing the z-score of the observation:

\[ Z = \frac{97 - 109}{4.73} = \frac{-12}{4.73} = -2.54 \]

Conclusion:

  • Observed height of a 5 y.o. girl falls below -2 standard deviations.

  • Thus she might be suffering from stunting if other causes of growth deprevation (e.g. genetic) are eliminated.
