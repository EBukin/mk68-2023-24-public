Causal interpretation of the MLR

Multiple Regression Controls for the factors that matter…

  • These are only the factors that are included in the model.

All other factors that are not included in the MLR are assumed to remain constant!

  • External Ceteris Paribus is assumed.

Violation of the Ceteris Paribus causes Bias.

Regression assumptions

They ensure that linear regression estimates obtained using the OLS method are unbiased and efficient.

  • OLS is unbiased: Assumptions 1 to 4 are satisfied

    1. Linearity
    2. Random Sampling
    3. No Collinearity
    4. No Endogeneity

  • OLS is unbiased and efficient or Best Linear Unbiased Estimator (BLUE)

    1. Homoscedasticity (No Autocorrelation)

  • OLS is a Classical linear model (CLM). Assumptions 1 to 6 are satisfied

Example 1. Hedonic Land prices model

1. Problem setting

We would like to assess the effect of the “Conservation Reserve Program” (CPR) on agricultural land prices.

Conservation Reserve Program

  • is a subsidy
  • obligates farms NOT TO GROW ANY CROPS on the enrolled land
  • pays monetary compensation in exchange;

This is a riskless income, so it may increase the land price.

  • However, if the land is productive, such set-aside measures may reduce farmland prices.

2. Regression model formulation

The hedonic price model (Palmquist 1989) is an econometric approach to quantifying monetary values of differentiated characteristics of goods or services, which are subjects of economic exchange.

The theory of the hedonic price model allows us to estimate the price as a function of all characteristics of a good. Available variables in our data (Taff and Weisberg 2007) are:

  • acrePrice - sale price in dollars per acre;
  • crpPct - the percentage of the farm area enrolled in CRP;
  • acres - the size of the farm in acres;
  • region - a region in the state of Minnesota;
  • tillable - the percentage of the farm that is rated arable by the assessor;
  • productivity - average agronomic productivity scaled from 1 to 100, with larger numbers for more productive land;

EQ: Write down a regression equation that could help answering the main research question.

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{acrePrice} &= \alpha + \beta_{1}(\operatorname{crpPct}) + \beta_{2}(\operatorname{acres})\ + \\ &\quad \beta_{3}(\operatorname{region}_{\operatorname{West\ Central}}) + \beta_{4}(\operatorname{region}_{\operatorname{Central}}) + \beta_{5}(\operatorname{region}_{\operatorname{South\ West}})\ + \\ &\quad \beta_{6}(\operatorname{region}_{\operatorname{South\ Central}}) + \beta_{7}(\operatorname{region}_{\operatorname{South\ East}}) + \beta_{8}(\operatorname{tillable})\ + \\ &\quad \beta_{9}(\operatorname{productivity}) + \epsilon \end{aligned} \]

Log transformation requires adjusting the interpretation:

\(\log(y) = \beta \log (x)\) is a log-log transformation

  • 1 % (percent) change in \(x\) causes \(\beta\) % (percent) change in \(y\) (AKA Elasticity).

\(\log(y) = \beta x\) is a log-level transformation

  • 1 unit change in \(x\) causes \(100 \beta\) % (percent) change in \(y\).

\(y = \beta \log(x)\) is a level-log transformation

  • 1 % (percent) change in \(x\) causes \(\beta / 100\) unit in \(y\).

Example 2. Production function

1. Problem setting

We would like to evaluate the causal effect of the BIMAS production intensification program on the rice output among rice-producing farmers in India.

  • The outcome variable is:

    • goutput - gross output of rice in kg per year produced on the farm.

  • Key variable of interest is:

    • bimas variable of interest is an intensification program. Each farmer either participates in it (yes), partially participate (mixed), or does not participate (no).

  • Other control variables are:

    • size the total area cultivated with rice, measured in hectares;
    • urea urea application in kilogram
    • phosphate phosphate application in kilogram
    • hiredlabor hired labor in hours
    • famlabor family labor in hours

2. Regression model

The production function is used to estimate the relationship. We consider two main production functions (see Feng and Horrace 2012):

  1. Linear production function, when output is a linear combination of all inputs. (Write it down)

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{goutput} &= \alpha + \beta_{1}(\operatorname{size}) + \beta_{2}(\operatorname{bimas}_{\operatorname{yes}})\ + \\ &\quad \beta_{3}(\operatorname{bimas}_{\operatorname{mixed}}) + \beta_{4}(\operatorname{famlabor}) + \beta_{5}(\operatorname{hiredlabor})\ + \\ &\quad \beta_{6}(\operatorname{urea}) + \beta_{7}(\operatorname{phosphate}) + \epsilon \end{aligned} \]

  1. Cobb-Douglas production function, when the log of output is a linear combination of logged inputs. (Write it down)

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{log(goutput)} &= \alpha + \beta_{1}(\operatorname{\log(size)}) + \beta_{2}(\operatorname{bimas}_{\operatorname{yes}})\ + \\ &\quad \beta_{3}(\operatorname{bimas}_{\operatorname{mixed}}) + \beta_{4}(\operatorname{\log(famlabor)}) + \beta_{5}(\operatorname{\log(hiredlabor)})\ + \\ &\quad \beta_{6}(\operatorname{\log(urea)}) + \beta_{7}(\operatorname{\log(phosphate)}) + \epsilon \end{aligned} \]

2.1 Ex-ante hypotheses about the coefficients

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{goutput} &= \alpha + \beta_{1}(\operatorname{size}) + \beta_{2}(\operatorname{bimas}_{\operatorname{yes}})\ + \\ &\quad \beta_{3}(\operatorname{bimas}_{\operatorname{mixed}}) + \beta_{4}(\operatorname{famlabor}) + \beta_{5}(\operatorname{hiredlabor})\ + \\ &\quad \beta_{6}(\operatorname{urea}) + \beta_{7}(\operatorname{phosphate}) + \epsilon \end{aligned} \]

Example 3. Duflo et al. (2008) How High Are Rates of Return to Fertilizer? Evidence from Field Experiments in Kenya

Objective

In (Duflo et al. 2008) authors are trying to estimate the effect of fertilizers application on farms profit.

They employ and randomized controlled trial experiment randomly selecting farmers between children’s parents in certain schools.

On each farm, they demarcate 3 experimental fields and train farmers how to apply certain quantities of fertilizers on these fields. They also supervised the farmers over the growing seasons accurately collected the harvest and measured the outcome each time.

In each trial, random plots (out of the demarcated ones) were selected on each farm for applying different levels of fertilizers.

The authors measured:

  • returns as [output value of (treatment - control) - inputs cost] / [inputs cost]
  • yields change (treatment - control) / (control) * 100

Results

Column (1) reports the mean in returns and yields between control and treatment fields for different rates of fertilizer application overall growing seasons.

  • What yield change is associated with different levels of fertilizer application?

  • Can we conclude that fertilizer application improves yields/returns on average in the population?

  • What rates of fertilizer application improve yields/returns on average in the population?

Is the ceteris paribus ensured in this RCT?

Authors were criticized if the ceteris paribus holds in their experiment

  • specifically concerning the monetary return levels.

  • monetary outcomes often depend on many observed and unobserved factors, therefore, they are often seen as “endogenous” in econometrics.

    • What other factors can affect the return?

    • How can we ensure that other factors do not affect the return?

Authors solution is the regression analysis

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{Return} &= \beta_0 + \beta_1 \operatorname{Farm size} + \beta_2 \operatorname{Education} + \beta_3 \operatorname{Income} \\ & + \beta_4 \operatorname{Experience in fertilizer use} + \cdots \\ & + \beta_k \operatorname{Other factors} + \epsilon \end{aligned} \]

If other factors affect the return,

  • What signs/values coefficients \(\beta_\cdot\) should have?

  • Formulate ex-ante hypotheses about the expected signs of the coefficients.
